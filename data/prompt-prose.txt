Write a first-person narrative account of a single night shift spent alone in a small coastal weather station as a storm closes in. The narrator is an experienced observer who has made this round many times, and the piece should trust the reader: favour concrete sensory detail and precise physical action over explanation, abstraction, or emotional commentary. Let feeling arrive only through what is noticed and what is done.

Ground the piece in the specific equipment and routine of the station: the instruments and their readings, the log book and the act of writing in it, the lamp and its limitations, the walk outside to check the exposed instruments, the state of the sea and the horizon, the radio and its silence or interference, and the particular quality of light and sound as the weather arrives. Include at least one moment where an instrument behaves unexpectedly and the narrator has to decide whether to trust it, and resolve that decision through a physical check rather than through reasoning alone.

Keep the voice restrained, observant, and unhurried. Use no dialogue and no quotation. Avoid weather clichés, avoid stating the weather's symbolic meaning, and avoid any sentence that tells the reader how to feel about what is happening. Structure the piece with an opening that establishes the routine, a turn where the storm forces a change in the routine, and a quiet ending that returns the narrator to some small ordinary task. Aim for a complete, self-contained piece.
